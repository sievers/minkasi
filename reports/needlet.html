Multi-resolution needlet mapmaking: status

Last updated 2026-10-20. Branch needlet, master merged.

New here? Step by step walkthrough of twores_ex.py, including what solve_bands does.

Method as implemented

For each needlet band i the noise precision is computed on a downsampled lattice of pixels, Gi = KiT O Ki, with Ki the band-limited real-space response of a unit coefficient. O is PTN-1P for the M2 TODs and a map-space inverse covariance for ACT. The combined coefficients solve (GM2+GACT)c = KT(rhsM2+rhsACT) per band; the map is wav2map_real of the zero-filled coefficients. Cross-band blocks are neglected (design of the project).

Speed-up of the response matrix

Old: one full TOD pass (plus ~2*nfilt full FFTs) per lattice pixel, dense (npixred x npix) float32; the committed run stored ~87 GB. New: impulses spaced further apart than a window of half width radius are probed together, so only ~(2*radius/downsamp)2 TOD passes per band are needed; single-band rfft transforms replace the full transforms; the result is a sparse symmetric matrix on the lattice. radius=None reproduces the exact matrix (tested against the original brute force transforms to 1e-6).

Combined map (MOOJ1142, 20 TODs)

PCG (M2 only, all scales, 50 iterations), ACT only (inverse variance weighted coadd of the splits), the combined correction (M2 ANA solved for all bands down to the 5″ scale, bands 0–8; ACT only in bands 0–3) and final = PCG + combined. Units are Jy/beam, the units of the M2 TODs. The final map is padded by 25% of the data extent on each side. The panels are not smoothed; pixels with fewer than 20 hits are filled with the ACT map (plotting only). A fifth panel shows the final map smoothed to the ACT beam on the ACT colour scale; colour limits elsewhere are the 1st–99th percentiles of unmasked pixels.

With 20 TODs the final map has a central rms (smoothed 6 px) of 5.3×10-5 against 2.3×10-4 for PCG alone, and, smoothed to the ACT beam, correlates with ACT at 0.91 inside the footprint (PCG alone: −0.61). The large-scale artifact of the PCG map (a ±0.5 mJy gradient from the poorly constrained large-scale modes) is removed by the combined solve. With only 2 TODs M2 is too noisy for the cold spot to show above the PCG noise (about 1 mJy per pixel).

Solving down to 5″

The M2 response is built for every band with extent above --max-res (default 5″; bands 0–8 at 3″ pixels), not just the coarse ones. Fine bands have large lattices (band 7: 360² = 129,600 points), so each band gets its own probing radius (twice the filter's real-space extent, capped by --radius), solve_bands switches to Jacobi-preconditioned CG above 20,000 points, and the response cache is per band. With 20 TODs the M2 response took 2400 s for bands 5–8 plus reuse of 0–4 caches, the solve 1090 s (60 min wall, 1 node), no CG convergence warnings. The final map is essentially unchanged relative to bands 0–3 only (central rms smoothed 6 px: 4.82×10-5 in both; difference rms 2.5×10-5): PCG already converges the small scales, and the fine bands only fix the noise weighting there.

Model of the ACT term

  • Units. ACT µKCMB are converted to Jy/beam (RJ) with act_conversion (thermodynamic to RJ temperature, matching witch.utils.K_CMB2K_RJ, times the M2 beam solid angle; 90 GHz and 9″ FWHM by default, 4.37×10-7 Jy/beam per µK). The TODs are taken to be in Jy: their median per sample noise is about 2, realistic for Jy/beam but not for mJy. With the factor 1000 wrong ACT was effectively weighted far too low against M2, which hid the cold spot (--m2-unit mJy switches back).
  • Residual. The combined solve is for the correction to the PCG map (already subtracted from the TODs), so ACT enters through ACT − (beam and pixel smoothed PCG map), not the absolute map.
  • Beam and pixel window. ACT sees the sky through a 2.1′ Gaussian beam (--act-beam) and 30″ pixels; the response matrix is KT B PT N-1 P B K.
  • Which bands. ACT is used in bands where the beam amplitude transfer is at least 5% (--act-min-transfer): transfers are 0.95, 0.79, 0.46, 0.09, 0.002 for bands 0–4, so bands 0–3. Dropping band 3 from the combined solve made the result worse (correlation with ACT 0.71, central rms 8.9×10-5, arcs of residual M2 artifact around the footprint), so band 3 is kept. The M2 and ACT precisions per band relative to each other show no band without information: M2/ACT diagonal ratio is 2×10-6, 8×10-5, 2×10-3, 5×10-2 in bands 0–3 with the M2 filter transfer of the ACT map 0.5–0.9.
  • Regularisation. Bands where ACT is weakly sensitive amplify its noise by about 1/transfer outside the M2 footprint; a ridge of 10-3 times the mean diagonal of G (solve_bands(ridge=)) acts as a zero-mean prior. A ridge relative to the median diagonal was singular for M2-only bands, where most pixels have no data.
  • ACT noise is the summed split ivar as a diagonal inverse covariance. TODO: replace by a robust map-space noise covariance estimate.

Remaining ringing in the periphery of the final map comes from band 3 (low ACT transfer, no M2 data outside the footprint) and from the periodic wrap of the filters.

Direct solve and running on all TODs

twores_ex.py --direct solves the mapmaking equation directly: the final map is wav2map_real(solve_bands(rhsM2 + rhsACT, responses)) with the right hand side of the full (unsubtracted) M2 data, the ACT term built from the ACT map itself rather than a residual, and ANA in all bands (--max-res is ignored). PCG is only run to clean the noise estimate (its model enters set_noise(dat=...) but is never subtracted from the data or added to the map). Without the flag the old behaviour (PCG + correction) is unchanged. The M2 hits mask (--min-hits 20) is applied in the mapmaking in both modes.

With 20 TODs the direct map has a pixel rms of 2.6×10-4 against 3.3×10-4 for PCG + correction, correlates with ACT (ACT beam, inside the footprint) at 0.85 against 0.90, and the two maps correlate at 0.92 after smoothing by 6 px; the difference is mostly confined to the low-hit rim.

Cost

Each probe is one full pass over the TODs (TodVec.dot); the number of passes per band is (2·ceil(radius/downsamp)+1)2, or one per lattice point for bands below --exact-below (default 5000). At 3″ pixels, 10 filters and radius 30, band 5 (3600 lattice points) is computed exactly and takes 3600 of the 6245 passes. About 76% of a pass is the noise model (apply_noise: two dense detector rotations and two FFTs per TOD); map2tod and tod2map (numba) are 2% and 9%. Threads scale poorly (4 TODs: 38 ms per pass on 1 thread, 22 ms on 8), but MPI over TODs scales almost perfectly (1 rank × 4 TODs: 38 ms; 8 ranks × 4 TODs: 49 ms) and gives the same maps as one process (differences ~10-9). Run with OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 NUMBA_NUM_THREADS=1 mpirun -n N python twores_ex.py ...; --ntods is per rank. Each rank holds the response matrices, about 6 GB peak while probing band 7, so memory limits N.

--exact-below 1000 probes band 5 instead (20 s instead of about 400 s at 20 TODs). Relative to the exact band 5 the final map has the same ACT correlation (0.845), a 6 px smoothed correlation of 0.9993 and a pixel level difference of 9% of the map rms, from a matrix error of about 1% of its largest entry (1 TOD benchmark).

Correction: MPI reduction bug, and the CG solve on 40 TODs

The amplitude difference below was mostly a bug. SkyMap.mpi_reduce chunked by nx*ny, so for maps above 1e5 pixels only band 0 of a wavelet map (shape (nfilt, nx, ny)) was summed over MPI ranks; the other bands of the rhs and of A x stayed rank-local. All 16-rank wavelet runs (including the 138 TOD direct map) are affected: rank 0 wrote maps whose fine bands had the rhs of its own ~1/15 of the TODs but the response of all of them. Fixed (chunks by map.size, regression test test_mpi_reduce_chunks_whole_wavelet_map). Single rank runs, the 4 rank 12″ controls and the PCG example maps were not affected. The block-diagonal findings below (shift and ridge) remain true for the block solve, but are secondary.

Other fixes: get_response_matrix now probes the masked operator (as the rhs and PCG), min_eigenvalue has a fixed start (same shift on every rank), block_solver factorizes once, and twores_ex.py --cg N solves with CG on the full M2 + ACT operator, preconditioned by the block solve.

Earlier analysis (superseded where it attributes the amplitude to the regularization)

Block solve: shift, ridge and the exact-matrix control

In the common pixels (hits ≥ 20) the pixel rms is 5.0e-5 (needlet), 2.0e-4 (mask=20 PCG) and 7.3e-4 (plain PCG) Jy/beam. The needlet map has 15× lower peak on the brightest compact source, 4× lower pixel-to-pixel noise and 5–7× lower amplitude at 0.5–2′ scales, i.e. it is not an overall scale factor. At the ACT scale (bands 0–3, where ACT constrains the map) its amplitude agrees with ACT (slope 1.09 after a 2′–8′ band-pass, correlation 0.97).

  • The probed response matrices of the fine bands (5–9) are not positive definite (smallest eigenvalue between −0.2 and −1.1 times the mean in-footprint diagonal; the eigenvectors lie entirely inside the footprint), so solve_bands adds a diagonal shift of that size on top of the 1e-3 ridge. Relative to the in-footprint diagonal the solution is multiplied by d/(d+shift): median 0.63–0.89 in bands 5–8 and 0.28 in band 9, with 10th percentile 0.03–0.4 in the shallow regions. Compact sources live in the finest bands and the shallow regions are damped most.
  • The exact response matrices do not fix this: a control run (4 TODs, 12″ pixels, exact ANA, with ACT) has a pixel rms 17× higher than the PCG map and is uncorrelated with it. The block diagonal solve Fi2Gi-1Fi2r treats each band on its own, so the rhs of a band has components in modes that Gi cannot constrain (e.g. removed by the common mode subtraction), and these are amplified by 1/ridge. PCG never excites such modes.

So the absolute scale of the fine bands in the direct map is set by the regularization, not the data. Candidates: couple neighbouring bands in the solve (the off diagonal blocks of the ANA), use a local rather than global shift, enforce positive definiteness of the probed matrices, or use the probed ANA as a preconditioner for CG on the full system.

Follow-up tests (examples/ridge_test.py: 4 TODs, 12″ pixels, M2 only, exact response matrices)

  • CG on the full operator with the block solve (ridge 1e-3) as preconditioner converges (residual 3e-3 after 20 and 7e-6 after 40 iterations with the masked G) to the PCG map: pixel rms 3.34e-4 vs 3.33e-4 after 100 PCG steps, band-pass correlation 1.000, slope 0.998. So the exact ANA does give the PCG map once the bands are coupled.
  • Block solve alone, ridge sweep (against that converged map): the noise grows as 1/ridge (pixel noise 2.5e-4 at ridge 0.1, 7.7e-4 at 1e-3, 0.2 at 1e-6; band-pass slope 1.05, 6.1, 493). No ridge reproduces the map: the best band-pass correlation is 0.88, at a ridge of 0.1.
  • Mask mismatch: get_response_matrix probes the unmasked real space map, while the rhs and PCG are masked, so G differs from the operator in use (|Ax−Gx|/|Gx| = 1.6e-3 here, 2.9e-15 for a masked probe). As a preconditioner this only slows convergence (residual 2.5e-5 vs 7e-6 after 40 iterations); as a direct solve it is a smaller effect than the block approximation. It should still be fixed.
  • The 50 step PCG map is not converged (band-pass slope 0.85); a plain 300 step PCG of the same problem diverged (rms 100× larger), not investigated.

Is the CG stagnation a problem? Convergence per scale

The relative residual of the 40 TOD CG solve flattens at 3–4e-4 (2.8e-4 after 80 iterations). Checks, all on the 40 TOD, mask 20 problem:

  • Symmetry (twores_ex.py --cg --symcheck, random wavelet vectors): M2, ACT and M2+ACT are symmetric to ~1e-18; the block preconditioner to 4e-11 and positive definite. So neither is the cause.
  • Consistent right hand side (--consistent 20: rhs replaced by A·x): the residual keeps falling, to ~5e-5 after 40 iterations against 4e-4 for the real rhs. The real rhs has a component outside the range of the operator that no iterate removes; it does not change the solution. The consistent rhs is smoother than the real one, so the gap is somewhat overstated.
  • Per scale convergence (--cg 80 --cg-save ..., cg_convergence.py): the map still changes, but mostly at large scales.

Sensitivity to the ACT weight

ACT enters the solve through the diagonal, white inverse variance ivar in bands 0–3 (beam transfer ≥ 0.05), everywhere on the map. That noise model ignores the CMB, which is correlated noise for the cluster signal, so the weight of ACT against M2 may be too high. Test (--act-ivar-scale, which multiplies the ACT inverse variance and its rhs): the 40 TOD CG run with the ACT weight reduced by 10 and 100 (needlets/actscale), against the unscaled run (cg40). Relative residual after 40 iterations: 4.0e-4 (1), 4.3e-4 (0.1), 1.8e-3 (0.01).

Correction of the ACT weight and the CMB as noise

Normalization. read_act gives the ACT inverse variance of an ACT pixel, repeated on its 10×10 fine pixels, and the ACT term sums over fine pixels, so every ACT pixel was counted 100 times: ACT was weighted 100× too strongly in all runs above. Now divided by ACT_OSAMP2 (the ACT response cache name carries _n10). The run with the corrected weight (CG residual 1.82e-3 after 40 iterations, 177 s) is identical in residual to the --act-ivar-scale 0.01 run above, as it should be.

CMB. --act-cmb S multiplies each Fourier mode of the ACT noise precision by Nl/(Nl + S Cl Bl2) (lensed CAMB Cl, Planck 2018; B the 2.1′ ACT beam; Nl from the median ivar), as diag(√ivar) W diag(√ivar); S = 0 is the white model. Not included: foregrounds, the ACT pixel window, non-stationarity beyond the √ivar factors. Median total ACT ivar in the field: 2.1e-3 µK-2 per 0.5′ pixel (22 µK), giving weights 1.7e-4 (l=500), 1.6e-3 (1000), 3.5e-2 (2000), 0.49 (3000), 1.0 (5000): the CMB makes ACT nearly uninformative on the scales of the footprint.

The central decrement (4 px smoothed, within 3′ of the centre) is converged by iteration 40 in both runs and the CMB weight makes it 19% deeper at every iteration, so that difference is not a convergence effect. The large scales are not converged: the map change between successive saved iterations is 13–20% in the 16–64 px bands and does not decrease from 40 to 200 iterations (figures below, change relative to the iteration 200 map), at 1% at the smallest scales and 4–8% at 4–16 px. The difference between c1 and c0 at the largest scales shrinks (0.65 to 0.42) because both maps still move. The residual is flat while these scales move, so it cannot be used to decide convergence there.

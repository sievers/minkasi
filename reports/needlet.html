Multi-resolution needlet mapmaking: status

Last updated 2026-10-20. Branch needlet, master merged.

Method as implemented

For each needlet band i the noise precision is computed on a downsampled lattice of pixels, Gi = KiT O Ki, with Ki the band-limited real-space response of a unit coefficient. O is PTN-1P for the M2 TODs and a map-space inverse covariance for ACT. The combined coefficients solve (GM2+GACT)c = KT(rhsM2+rhsACT) per band; the map is wav2map_real of the zero-filled coefficients. Cross-band blocks are neglected (design of the project).

Speed-up of the response matrix

Old: one full TOD pass (plus ~2*nfilt full FFTs) per lattice pixel, dense (npixred x npix) float32; the committed run stored ~87 GB. New: impulses spaced further apart than a window of half width radius are probed together, so only ~(2*radius/downsamp)2 TOD passes per band are needed; single-band rfft transforms replace the full transforms; the result is a sparse symmetric matrix on the lattice. radius=None reproduces the exact matrix (tested against the original brute force transforms to 1e-6).

Full-size example (twores_ex.py, 3″ pixels, 218×218, 2 TODs, 10 filters, ANA for scales > 60″, radius 30): M2 ANA 15 s, ACT 2 s, whole script ~1 min, versus an 87 GB dense cache before.

Combined map (MOOJ1142, 20 TODs)

PCG (M2 only, all scales, 50 iterations), ACT only (inverse variance weighted coadd of the splits), the combined correction (M2 ANA solved for all bands down to the 5″ scale, bands 0–8; ACT only in bands 0–3) and final = PCG + combined. Units are Jy/beam, the units of the M2 TODs. The final map is padded by 25% of the data extent on each side. The panels are not smoothed; pixels with fewer than 20 hits are masked.

With 20 TODs the final map has a central rms (smoothed 6 px) of 5.3×10-5 against 2.3×10-4 for PCG alone, and, smoothed to the ACT beam, correlates with ACT at 0.91 inside the footprint (PCG alone: −0.61). The large-scale artifact of the PCG map (a ±0.5 mJy gradient from the poorly constrained large-scale modes) is removed by the combined solve. With only 2 TODs M2 is too noisy for the cold spot to show above the PCG noise (about 1 mJy per pixel).

Solving down to 5″

The M2 response is built for every band with extent above --max-res (default 5″; bands 0–8 at 3″ pixels), not just the coarse ones. Fine bands have large lattices (band 7: 360² = 129,600 points), so each band gets its own probing radius (twice the filter's real-space extent, capped by --radius), solve_bands switches to Jacobi-preconditioned CG above 20,000 points, and the response cache is per band. With 20 TODs the M2 response took 2400 s for bands 5–8 plus reuse of 0–4 caches, the solve 1090 s (60 min wall, 1 node), no CG convergence warnings. The final map is essentially unchanged relative to bands 0–3 only (central rms smoothed 6 px: 4.82×10-5 in both; difference rms 2.5×10-5): PCG already converges the small scales, and the fine bands only fix the noise weighting there.

Model of the ACT term

  • Units. ACT µKCMB are converted to Jy/beam (RJ) with act_conversion (thermodynamic to RJ temperature, matching witch.utils.K_CMB2K_RJ, times the M2 beam solid angle; 90 GHz and 9″ FWHM by default, 4.37×10-7 Jy/beam per µK). The TODs are taken to be in Jy: their median per sample noise is about 2, realistic for Jy/beam but not for mJy. With the factor 1000 wrong ACT was effectively weighted far too low against M2, which hid the cold spot (--m2-unit mJy switches back).
  • Residual. The combined solve is for the correction to the PCG map (already subtracted from the TODs), so ACT enters through ACT − (beam and pixel smoothed PCG map), not the absolute map.
  • Beam and pixel window. ACT sees the sky through a 2.1′ Gaussian beam (--act-beam) and 30″ pixels; the response matrix is KT B PT N-1 P B K.
  • Which bands. ACT is used in bands where the beam amplitude transfer is at least 5% (--act-min-transfer): transfers are 0.95, 0.79, 0.46, 0.09, 0.002 for bands 0–4, so bands 0–3. Dropping band 3 from the combined solve made the result worse (correlation with ACT 0.71, central rms 8.9×10-5, arcs of residual M2 artifact around the footprint), so band 3 is kept. The M2 and ACT precisions per band relative to each other show no band without information: M2/ACT diagonal ratio is 2×10-6, 8×10-5, 2×10-3, 5×10-2 in bands 0–3 with the M2 filter transfer of the ACT map 0.5–0.9.
  • Regularisation. Bands where ACT is weakly sensitive amplify its noise by about 1/transfer outside the M2 footprint; a ridge of 10-3 times the mean diagonal of G (solve_bands(ridge=)) acts as a zero-mean prior. A ridge relative to the median diagonal was singular for M2-only bands, where most pixels have no data.
  • ACT noise is the summed split ivar as a diagonal inverse covariance. TODO: replace by a robust map-space noise covariance estimate.

Remaining ringing in the periphery of the final map comes from band 3 (low ACT transfer, no M2 data outside the footprint) and from the periodic wrap of the filters.
